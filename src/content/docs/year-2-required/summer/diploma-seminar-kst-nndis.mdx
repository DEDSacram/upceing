# Diploma Seminar (KST/NNDIS)

The Diploma Seminar (2 credits, completion: Zp) accompanies your thesis work. Its purpose is to present your progress, receive feedback, and practice the defense. There are no weekly lecture topics — the seminar is driven by your presentations.

### Course Objectives

- Present thesis goals, progress, and results clearly and concisely.
- Receive and incorporate feedback from teachers and peers.
- Practice academic discussion and defense-style Q&A.
- Keep thesis work on schedule through regular milestones.

## Seminar Requirements

<div class="exercise-box">

<strong>📋 To receive credit, students typically must:</strong>

1. Attend seminar sessions as scheduled by the guarantor.
2. Deliver **at least one progress presentation** on their thesis topic.
3. Submit supporting materials (slides and/or a short progress report) as required.
4. Incorporate feedback and demonstrate progress toward the thesis goals.

</div>

> **Note:** Exact requirements (number of presentations, report length, deadlines) are set by the seminar guarantor each year. Confirm them in the first session.

### Typical Presentation Slots

| Presentation | Timing | Content |
|--------------|--------|---------|
| Topic + goals | Early semester | Assignment, motivation, planned approach |
| Progress report | Mid semester | Design, partial implementation, problems met |
| Results + defense rehearsal | Late semester | Results, evaluation, demo, conclusions |

## Presentation Checklist

<div class="checklist">

- [ ] Title slide: thesis title, name, supervisor
- [ ] One slide: problem and motivation (why it matters)
- [ ] One slide: goals from the official assignment
- [ ] State of the art in 1–2 slides (key sources, related solutions)
- [ ] Design / architecture (one clear diagram)
- [ ] Implementation status (what works, what remains)
- [ ] Evaluation plan or preliminary results
- [ ] Timeline and next steps
- [ ] Slides readable (large font, no walls of text)
- [ ] Talk rehearsed within the time limit
- [ ] Demo prepared, with screenshots/video as backup

</div>

### Presentation Tips

- Aim for **8–12 minutes** plus discussion, unless instructed otherwise.
- One idea per slide; prefer diagrams and tables over paragraphs.
- End with concrete next steps — this invites useful feedback.
- Note every question and comment; bring a written response to the next session.

## How to Link With the Thesis

The seminar and the thesis ([Diploma Thesis — KST/NNDIP](./diploma-thesis-kst-nndip/)) are two courses but one body of work:

1. **Use seminar deadlines as thesis milestones** — e.g. progress talk = design chapter done.
2. **Recycle seminar slides** into the defense deck.
3. **Turn feedback into thesis text** — reviewer questions reveal weak chapters.
4. **Keep both in sync** — if your topic or scope changes, inform both the supervisor and the seminar guarantor.

<div class="exercise-box">

<strong>🎯 Task:</strong> Map your seminar talks to thesis chapters.

- Talk 1 → Introduction + goals + literature plan.
- Talk 2 → Design + implementation status.
- Talk 3 → Results + conclusions + defense rehearsal.
- After each talk, write one paragraph summarising the feedback and what you changed.

</div>

---

## Common Pitfalls

- **Reading slides verbatim** — talk to the audience; slides are cues, not a script.
- **No clear status** — always separate "done", "in progress", and "planned".
- **Hiding problems** — reporting blockers early earns help; hiding them earns delay.
- **Scope creep** — agree any scope change with the supervisor before the next talk.
- **Missing backup** — demos fail; screenshots or a recorded video save the presentation.

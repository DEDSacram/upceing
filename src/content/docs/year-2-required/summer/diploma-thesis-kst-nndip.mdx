### Course Objectives

- Carry out an independent, larger-scale engineering or research task.
- Demonstrate ability to survey literature and describe the state of the art.
- Design and implement a working solution (software, system, analysis, or prototype).
- Evaluate the solution experimentally and draw conclusions.
- Document the work in a formal thesis and defend it.

## Key Tasks

### 1. Literature Review and State of the Art

- Survey books, papers, documentation, and existing tools related to your topic.
- Compare at least 2–3 existing solutions or approaches.
- Summarise findings in a structured chapter with proper citations.
- Keep a bibliography from day one (BibTeX, Zotero, or similar).

### 2. Practical Design and Implementation

- Derive functional and non-functional requirements from the assignment.
- Produce an analysis/design (architecture, data model, UML where appropriate).
- Implement iteratively; commit regularly with meaningful messages.
- Track decisions and changes in a short development log.

### 3. Experimental Testing and Evaluation

- Define test scenarios, datasets, or evaluation criteria early.
- Run functional tests, measurements, or user evaluation depending on topic type.
- Present results with tables or charts and interpret them honestly.
- Document limitations and threats to validity.

### 4. Defense Presentation Preparation

- Prepare a 10–15 minute slide deck (problem, approach, results, demo).
- Rehearse answers to likely questions (see [Defense Preparation](#defense-preparation)).
- Prepare a live demo or recorded backup.

## Structure Guide

A typical technical diploma thesis at KST has this structure:

1. **Title page, assignment, declaration** — use the official faculty templates.
2. **Abstract (Czech + English)** — problem, method, results in ~150–250 words each.
3. **Introduction** — motivation, goals, thesis outline.
4. **Theoretical part / state of the art** — literature review, technologies, related work.
5. **Analysis and design** — requirements, architecture, data model, algorithm design.
6. **Implementation** — key modules, technologies, interesting problems solved.
7. **Testing and evaluation** — methodology, results, discussion.
8. **Conclusion** — summary, fulfilled goals, future work.
9. **Bibliography** — consistent citation style (ISO 690 or as required).
10. **Appendices** — user manual, installation guide, CD/repository contents, listings.

### Writing Tips

- Write continuously — do not leave all writing to the end.
- One chapter = one idea flow; use figures and tables with captions and references.
- Every claim or borrowed idea needs a citation.
- Run a spell-check and have someone else proofread the final text.

## Defense Preparation

<div class="exercise-box">

<strong>🎯 Task:</strong> Prepare and rehearse your defense talk.

1. Build 10–14 slides: assignment, goals, analysis, design, demo, results, conclusion.
2. Time the talk to fit the official limit (usually 10–15 minutes).
3. Prepare a demo with a fallback (screenshots or video) in case of technical issues.
4. Anticipate questions: why this technology, alternatives considered, how testing was done, what you would improve.

</div>

### Likely Committee Questions

- What is the main contribution of your thesis?
- Why did you choose this architecture / technology over alternatives?
- How did you verify correctness or quality of the solution?
- What are the limits of your solution and what would you do next?
